\begin{abstract}
Large Language Models (LLMs) evaluated predominantly on English benchmarks frequently suffer from catastrophic factual hallucination when deployed in typologically distinct, morphologically rich, and lower-resource languages. In Bengali---the fifth most spoken native language globally, with over 300 million speakers---factual hallucination dynamics remain largely uncharacterized across formal versus code-mixed registers and high-stakes domains. We introduce \textbf{Matibhrom (\foreignlanguage{bengali}{মতিভ্রম})}, the first fine-grained, multi-condition hallucination benchmark for Bengali. Matibhrom comprises 600 expert-verified base items (1,200 matched prompts in native Standard Bangla and Romanized Banglish) grounded across a frozen corpus of 1,800 gold and distractor passages spanning General, Medical, and Legal domains. We evaluate small open-weight language models across four controlled grounding conditions: Closed-book ($C_0$), Oracle Grounding ($C_1$), Retrieval-Augmented Generation with Dense Distractors ($C_2$), and Selective Abstention Guardrailing ($C_3$). Across 480 matched generations with paired McNemar significance tests and 1,000-sample bootstrap 95\% confidence intervals, our empirical findings reveal:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Linguistic Register Degradation ($p < 0.001$):} Latin-transliterated Banglish severely disperses multilingual embeddings, slashing dense retrieval recall by more than half (from 66.7\% to 30.0\%) and triggering a statistically significant 3.27-fold surge in generative hallucination on Gemma 4B ($\chi^2 = 19.862, p = 8.32 \times 10^{-6}$).
    \item \textbf{Parametric Ignorance vs.\ Extractive Competence ($p < 10^{-7}$):} In closed-book settings ($C_0$), models exhibit near-total parametric absence (98.3\% hallucination). Supplying oracle evidence ($C_1$) slashes hallucination by more than half ($p < 10^{-7}$ across models), establishing that Bengali LLMs possess high extractive reading comprehension despite impoverished parametric memory.
    \item \textbf{Emergent Selective Abstention under Model Scaling ($p = 0.0004$):} While dense distractors induce acute hallucination rebounds in $C_2$ (58.3\%--73.3\%), selective abstention instructions ($C_3$) reveal a striking scaling divergence: Qwen 1.7B struggles with negative constraints (28.3\% hallucination), whereas Gemma 4B achieves near-total hallucination eradication (\textbf{1.7\% hallucination rate}, 83.3\% abstention rate, and \textbf{90.0\% selective accuracy}; $\chi^2 = 12.500, p = 0.0004$).
    \item \textbf{High-Stakes Domain Disparity:} Legal queries demonstrate the highest hallucination vulnerability (61.3\%--78.7\%) due to cross-jurisdictional transposition of foreign legal precedents, whereas Medical queries achieve the highest evidence adherence.
\end{enumerate}
Finally, we demonstrate that automated zero-shot LLM-as-a-judge evaluators fail to achieve reliable inter-annotator agreement on low-resource Bengali ($\kappa \le 0.34$), establishing the necessity of deterministic gold-evidence verification for trustworthy multilingual evaluation. All benchmark items, retrieval corpora, codebook schemas, and evaluation scripts are publicly released to support reproducible research.
\end{abstract}
