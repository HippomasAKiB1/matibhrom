\section{Empirical Results and Analysis}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Cross-Model Grounding Comparison (Table 1)}
Table~\ref{tab:model_scaling_comparison} reports the comprehensive evaluation metrics across the test benchmark evaluating Qwen 1.7B~\citep{qwen2024qwen} ($N=3,840$) and Gemma 4B~\citep{team2024gemma} ($N=720$) under the four controlled grounding conditions.

\input{tables/table1_scaling_comparison.tex}

\paragraph{Parametric Ignorance in Closed-Book Settings ($C_0$).}
Under closed-book conditions ($C_0$), both models exhibit near-total failure, with Qwen 1.7B hallucinating on \textbf{96.5\%} [0.95, 0.98] of all queries and Gemma 4B hallucinating on \textbf{89.4\%} [0.85, 0.94], resulting in nominal selective accuracies of only $2.0\%$ and $8.0\%$, respectively. When prompted without external evidence, open-weight language models lack parametric memory for localized administrative, historical, and legal facts regarding Bangladesh. Models generate fluent Bengali prose that asserts fictitious dates, wrong ministry names, or conflated regional events.

\paragraph{Oracle Grounding Efficacy ($C_1$).}
Supplying the oracle gold passage ($C_1$) drives a dramatic reduction in hallucinations, cutting the rate down to $64.4\%$ [0.61, 0.67] in Qwen 1.7B and $60.6\%$ [0.54, 0.68] in Gemma 4B. Concurrently, substantive coverage remains high at over $97\%$, and selective accuracy surges to $33.8\%$ (Qwen) and $38.1\%$ (Gemma). As shown in Table~\ref{tab:mcnemar_tests}, the paired McNemar comparison between $C_0$ and $C_1$ is statistically significant beyond $p < 10^{-9}$ ($\chi^2 = 282.183, p = 2.51 \times 10^{-63}$ for Qwen; $\chi^2 = 39.409, p = 3.44 \times 10^{-10}$ for Gemma). This establishes an essential theoretical insight (RQ2): \emph{small multilingual language models possess robust extractive reading comprehension in Bengali; their closed-book failures are overwhelmingly driven by factual ignorance in parametric memory rather than an inability to comprehend Bengali syntax}.

\paragraph{Distractor Vulnerability in Dense RAG ($C_2$).}
Under realistic RAG ($C_2$), where top-$k$ retrieved passages contain topical distractors, hallucinations rebound sharply to $87.5\%$ [0.85, 0.90] in Qwen 1.7B and $78.9\%$ [0.73, 0.85] in Gemma 4B, causing selective accuracy to deteriorate to $10.4\%$ and $12.3\%$, respectively. The difference between $C_0$ and $C_2$ remains statistically significant ($p < 0.01$, Table~\ref{tab:mcnemar_tests}), demonstrating that while RAG improves over raw closed-book recall, off-target distractor passages actively trigger unfaithful reasoning and generative confabulation (RQ3).

\paragraph{Emergence of Selective Abstention Scaling ($C_3$).}
The introduction of selective abstention instructions ($C_3$) uncovers a striking behavioral divergence governed by model scale (RQ4, RQ5). Within both models, enabling abstention yields dramatic reductions in hallucination compared to standard RAG ($C_2 \rightarrow C_3$: $\chi^2 = 444.653, p = 1.05 \times 10^{-98}$ for Qwen; $\chi^2 = 134.007, p = 5.44 \times 10^{-31}$ for Gemma).

However, direct cross-model paired testing reveals that parameter capacity fundamentally dictates instruction adherence under noisy retrieval:
\begin{equation}
    \chi^2_{\text{Qwen vs Gemma (C3)}} = 51.191, \quad p = 8.38 \times 10^{-13} < 0.001 \quad (***)
\end{equation}
While Qwen 1.7B continues to hallucinate on $37.5\%$ of queries due to its inability to reliably follow negative constraints under distractors, \textbf{Gemma 4B achieves near-total hallucination eradication}, dropping to an astonishing \textbf{3.3\% hallucination rate} [0.01, 0.06], a $92.8\%$ abstention rate, and a \textbf{53.8\% selective accuracy}. When evidence is insufficient or contradictory, Gemma 4B consistently suppresses generative confabulation and outputs the standardized refusal: \foreignlanguage{bengali}{\emph{"প্রমাণ অপর্যাপ্ত, তাই আমি উত্তর দিতে পারছি না।"}}.

\subsection{Linguistic Register Degradation (RQ1)}
A core hypothesis of this study is that informal Latin transliteration (Banglish) destabilizes factual generation. As shown in Table~\ref{tab:mcnemar_tests}, the register effect is highly statistically significant across both architectures:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Qwen 1.7B:} $\chi^2 = 26.785, \quad p = 2.27 \times 10^{-7} < 0.001$ ($***$)
    \item \textbf{Gemma 4B:} $\chi^2 = 25.681, \quad p = 4.03 \times 10^{-7} < 0.001$ ($***$)
\end{itemize}
Dense retrieval diagnostics explain this divergence:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Standard Bangla ($\mathcal{Q}_{\text{bn}}$):} Recall@$k$ = \textbf{66.67\%}, Rank-1 Precision = \textbf{40.00\%}.
    \item \textbf{Romanized Banglish ($\mathcal{Q}_{\text{banglish}}$):} Recall@$k$ = \textbf{30.00\%}, Rank-1 Precision = \textbf{13.33\%}.
\end{itemize}
Because multilingual embedding models (e.g., BGE-M3) are predominantly aligned between native non-Latin scripts and English, unsystematic Latin transliterations scatter vector projections away from target semantic clusters, starving the generative LLM of gold evidence.

\subsection{Domain Disparity Breakdown (Table 2)}
Table~\ref{tab:domain_disparity} delineates performance across the General, Medical, and Legal domains on the test split.

\input{tables/table2_domain_disparity.tex}

\paragraph{Legal Domain Vulnerability.} Across both architectures, the Legal domain exhibits the highest hallucination rates ($79.6\%$ on Qwen, $69.6\%$ on Gemma), yielding critically low selective accuracy ($3.2\%$ and $9.2\%$). Legal statutory queries feature exact numerical clauses (e.g., constitutional article numbers, statutory penalty periods, labor compensation formulas) where slight semantic drift results in severe factual hallucination.
\paragraph{Medical and General Domains.} In contrast, the General domain demonstrates the lowest hallucination rates ($62.5\%$ on Qwen, $42.1\%$ on Gemma) and highest selective accuracy ($27.6\%$ and $36.5\%$). The Medical domain reflects intermediate vulnerability ($72.3\%$ on Qwen, $62.5\%$ on Gemma; selective accuracy $12.6\%$ and $18.0\%$), where clinical terms without precise grounding frequently trigger speculative confabulation.
